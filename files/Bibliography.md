## Readings on publishing

###  Industry structure and economics
- Thompson, John B. *Merchants of Culture: The Publishing Business in the Twenty-First Century*. 2nd ed. Cambridge: Polity, 2012. This is the standard account of trade publishing as a field. Thompson builds on Bourdieu, so it pairs well with your Creative Labour II session.
- Thompson, John B. *Book Wars: The Digital Revolution in Publishing*. Cambridge: Polity, 2021. It covers ebooks, Amazon, self-publishing, crowdfunding and reading platforms such as Wattpad. It is the best single book on publishing and the web.
- Bourdieu, Pierre. 'A Conservative Revolution in Publishing'. *Translation Studies* 1, no. 2, 2008: 123–53. This is a short, empirical application of field theory to French publishing houses.
- Sinykin, Dan. *Big Fiction: How Conglomeration Changed the Publishing Industry and American Literature*. New York: Columbia University Press, 2023. It argues that corporate ownership has shaped what gets written, and its chapters work well on their own.
- *United States v. Bertelsmann SE & Co. KGaA*, Memorandum Opinion, D.D.C., 2022. This is Judge Florence Pan's ruling blocking Penguin Random House's purchase of Simon & Schuster. It is a primary source, and it continues your existing Bertelsmann case study. The opening 22 pages of the opinion lay out publishing industry practices, and the case focused on the buy side of the market, the acquisition of authors' publishing rights, not the sale of books.
- Association of American Publishers. *StatShot Annual Report*. This is useful for current figures. The 2025 report estimates that the U.S. publishing industry generated $33.4 billion in aggregate revenue for books and course materials.

###  History of the book
- Darnton, Robert. 'What Is the History of Books?' *Daedalus* 111, no. 3, 1982: 65–83. The 'communications circuit' it introduces is a model students can apply to any publishing system, including web platforms.
- Johns, Adrian. *Piracy: The Intellectual Property Wars from Gutenberg to Gates*. Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 2009. Selected chapters would connect with your session on intellectual property.
- Striphas, Ted. *The Late Age of Print: Everyday Book Culture from Consumerism to Control*. New York: Columbia University Press, 2009. It discusses Amazon, the ISBN, Oprah's Book Club and digital rights management, and it reads easily.

###  Publishing on the web and platforms
- Nieborg, David B., and Thomas Poell. 'The Platformization of Cultural Production: Theorizing the Contingent Cultural Commodity'. *New Media & Society* 20, no. 11, 2018: 4275–92. This is the key theoretical article on the topic.
- Giblin, Rebecca, and Cory Doctorow. *Chokepoint Capitalism*. Boston: Beacon Press, 2022. The chapters on Amazon and Audible are directly relevant, and the book suits your Platforms session.
- McGurl, Mark. *Everything and Less: The Novel in the Age of Amazon*. London: Verso, 2021. It is a literary critic's reading of Kindle Direct Publishing and genre fiction as a service.
- Fitzpatrick, Kathleen. *Planned Obsolescence: Publishing, Technology, and the Future of the Academy*. New York: NYU Press, 2011. It covers open access and peer review online, and an open version is available.
- De Kosnik, Abigail. *Rogue Archives: Digital Cultural Memory and Media Fandom*. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2016. It deals with fan fiction archives such as AO3 and connects with your Brands session.
- Duffy, Brooke Erin. *(Not) Getting Paid to Do What You Love: Gender, Social Media, and Aspirational Work*. New Haven: Yale University Press, 2017. It covers creator labour in blogging and online publishing.


- Ludovico, Alessandro. *Post-Digital Print: The Mutation of Publishing since 1894*. Eindhoven: Onomatopee, 2012. It covers zines, artists' books and the relationship between print and the web, and a free PDF is available.
- Gilbert, Annette, ed. *Publishing as Artistic Practice*. Berlin: Sternberg Press, 2016.
